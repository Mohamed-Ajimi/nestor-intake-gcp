Both were reproduced on the merged tree before this plan was written; neither is theoretical.

**WR-01 — mandate-strict is a NO-OP at the ceiling.** `clamp_groups` measures
`room = ceiling - len(work)` BEFORE the merge pass. A model returning five groups at a ceiling of
five gets `room = 0`, so the D-W3-5 single-parent split never runs. Measured on the real module at
`8a6c59f`: a proposal of five groups where one carries `{Q1, Q2}` and another carries `{Q1}` comes
back with the mixed group **intact** — and carrying the note *"A group had to keep questions from 2
different client questions because splitting it would have needed more than the 5 groups this run
allows."* That sentence is **false**: merging the two Q1 groups would have freed the slot. So the
defect ships twice — as the wrong grouping, and as a run-report note that misstates why.

The consequence is not cosmetic. A mixed group gives every one of its claims the top-ranked member's
`facet`, because the D8 fact-list contract has no per-fact facet column — so claims answering the
other client question are attributed to the wrong one, in the run this phase exists to measure.

**WR-05 — `0 or 1` buys a group the operator did not authorise.** `group_winners` resolves
`ceiling = max(1, int(max_groups or 1))`. `workshop_rank` computes
`max_mandate_groups = _D6_MAX_GROUPS - (1 if cross_cutting else 0)`, so `NESTOR_TRIBUNAL_D6_MAX_GROUPS=1`
plus a cross-cutting question hands this function a **legitimate 0** — and `0 or 1` cannot tell that
apart from an absent value. The mandate gets a group anyway, `d1` is appended after it, and a dial the
operator set to one group (three paid calls) dispatches two groups (six).

Purpose: both defects distort the ONE measuring run, and there is no second run to correct it.
Output: a clamp whose ceiling is enforced after the merge, a ceiling resolver that can read zero, and
seven fixture-free tests — four of them sitting AT the ceiling, where the defect is visible.
</objective>
