## 4. The operator Q&A — carry these forward

### 4.0 Architecture: the report is written from the ESSAYS, not the claims

The single most clarifying finding of the session, and it changes how several numbers read.

`synthesize_report` (`synthesis/steps.py:934`) takes `provider_reports` and concatenates them:

```python
for name, result in provider_reports:
    report_blocks.append(f"### Provider: {name}\n\n{(result or {}).get('report') or ''}")
reports_concatenated = "\n\n---\n\n".join(report_blocks)
```

The claim table is **not** the input. Verification is **subtractive** — from the scrubber's own
module comment:

> *"Removes passages that state/depend on discredited claims … from the FULL research prose, then
> synthesis runs on the cleaned research. **This is what makes verification 'stick': a dropped
> claim cannot ride back into the report through the raw research.**"*

```
providers → full prose → distil 253 claims FROM the prose
   → gate → verify → adjudicate → conflict → 12 discredited
   → scrub_research DELETES their passages from the prose
   → synthesis runs on CLEANED prose + fact ledger + numbered citations + contested notes
```

`pipeline.py:4068` confirms it: `provider_reports=cleaned_reports`. The scrubber is hardened —
the LLM only *proposes* verbatim spans, Python deletes them, and a third deterministic layer
removes any surviving evidence snippet and logs loudly. **It ran on this run**: the report states
*"Removed after failing fact-checking or losing a conflict: 12."*

**Three consequences:**
1. Citations reach `[1362]` against 241 claims because they index **sources** (1,644), not claims.
2. `both: 0` does **not** degrade the prose — corroboration operates on claims, and claims are not
   the report's substance. The report gets its confidence signal a different way (§ 4.2).
3. The 53% not-load-bearing drop rate is a **coverage-of-verification** number, not content loss.
   Dropped claims keep their prose; they simply were not fact-checked.
