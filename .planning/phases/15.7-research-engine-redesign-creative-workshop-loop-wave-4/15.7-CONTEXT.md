### D-W4-1 — The rejected register is WITHIN-RUN ONLY

**Operator decision, 2026-07-31**, closing open item 3 of `15.7-OPEN-ITEMS.md`.

The spec's phrase *"barred this run, **kept for the next**"* implied cross-run storage. **It does not
mean that.** The register lives for the duration of **one workshop run** and dies with it. *"The
next"* means the next **round**, not the next run.

**Code-grounded fact:** `grep -n "barred" workshop_rank.py workshop.py` returns **nothing**. There is
no rejected register in the codebase at all today — not cross-run, not within-run. **Wave 4 builds it
from scratch.**

**Consequence: NO new table, NO fourth alembic migration.** Migrations `0016` and `0017` have still
never touched a database; this phase must not add a third unpaid proof.

**What the register holds**, per the spec's table — and the last row is load-bearing:

| Outcome | Treatment |
|---|---|
| `KILL` — unanswerable in principle / pure opinion / nothing turns on it | **barred** — it is a defect, and a reworded version has the same defect |
| `KILL` — restatement of another candidate | **NOT barred** — a duplicate is not a fault; the surviving twin represents it |
| `WEAK` after two evolve passes | **barred** — a real question the workshop could not sharpen |
| **Lost the tournament** | **NEVER barred** — it was fine, it just missed the cut |

**Bar a loser and you break `enforce_scope_guard`** (`workshop_rank.py:2130`), whose documented repair
order **promotes a below-the-cut candidate** when a client question has no winner. The coverage
guarantee depends on losers staying available.

**Enforcement is two layers, because the prompt layer will not hold.** In the prompt: the barred list
travels with every generate and evolve call, **each entry carrying its flaw** — *"don't propose these,
and here is why"* beats a bare list. In code: reuse `cluster_candidates` (`workshop.py:1482`),
clustering each round's new candidates **together with** the barred ones and dropping anything that
clusters onto a barred entry. Semantic, not string matching — which is what *"don't rephrase it"*
requires.

**A measured gap this must close:** in the harness, **failed-lookup angles were not added to the
barred register**, so *"minimale netwerkdichtheid"* was re-proposed in rounds 2 **and** 3, spending a
grounded lookup each time. **A dropped invention is a bar.**

**Log every drop.** *"Round 2 proposed 3 questions already rejected in round 1"* is the signal that the
loop is spinning rather than exploring. The harness also measured the **opposite** failure: the
semantic dedup dropped 6 proposals as rewordings — mostly fairly, but it also killed SPECIALISE and
COMBINE attempts, and it killed round 1's only INVENT **before the grounded lookup ever ran**. An
over-eager dedup suppresses discovery invisibly, so the drop log must record **what** was dropped and
**onto what**, not just a count.

### D-W4-2 — A discovery question's evolve enrichment is ITS OWN ANCHOR

**Operator decision, 2026-07-31**, closing open item 4 of `15.7-OPEN-ITEMS.md`.

Evolve is specified to receive *"the orientation findings for the parent question"* so it can inject
specificity. A discovery question's parent is **`__discovery__`**, which has no orientation findings —
the same class of gap `D-W3-5` closed for dispatch in Wave 3, and sharper now that D-R10 lets evolve
invent an angle in **any** round.

**The rule:** a discovery candidate **already carries the quote and URL that admitted it** through the
grounded lookup. **That anchor IS its findings block.** Self-consistent with *"no source, no slot"* —
the evidence that admitted the angle is the evidence that enriches it.

**For a cross-cutting question spanning two client questions, pass BOTH parents' orientation
findings.** Cross-cutting mandate questions are where the best measured output came from (see D-W4-5),
and they have two real parents, not `__discovery__`.

**Explicitly rejected:** passing the union of all orientation findings to any `__discovery__` parent.
It re-couples discovery to orientation — exactly the coupling D-R10 broke — and inflates the prompt.

### D-W4-3 — D-R11's median seed is REPLACED by a catch-up schedule

**Operator decision, 2026-07-31.** The spec (§ 5, `D-R11`) explicitly asks that this substitution be
routed to the operator rather than swapped silently. It was, and it is ruled.

**The "Elo carries" half STANDS unchanged.** Ratings persist across loop rounds. A reset would discard
eight rounds of accumulated judgement every round.

**The median-seed half is REPLACED.** It is not wrong, it is a **no-op** — which is worse, because it
reads as a solved problem. **Code-grounded:** `workshop_rank.py:1524` sorts by `(-wins, -elo, index)`
— **wins is primary, Elo is only the tie-break** — and `_apply_elo`'s own docstring at
`workshop_rank.py:846`ff says so in capitals: *"ELO IS THE TIE-BREAK, NOT THE PRIMARY KEY."* A
newcomer's disadvantage is **fewer matches and therefore fewer wins**, not a lower rating.
Median-seed and flat-1200 produce **byte-identical** output.

**The replacement rule: a new candidate plays up to the field's MEDIAN MATCH COUNT on entry**, then
the existing ranking runs **completely unmodified**. Measured with a perfect judge, 8 rounds, newcomer
entering round 6:

| standing rule | STRONG newcomer | MEDIAN | WEAK |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-R11 as ruled (median seed) | **1.5%** | 1.5% | 0.0% |
| flat 1200 seed | **byte-identical to median seed** | | |
| rank by raw win-*rate* (the obvious repair) | 95.5% | **93.8% — stops discriminating** | 5.8% |
| **catch-up schedule, sort UNCHANGED** | **99.8%** | **29.5%** | **1.8%** |

That last row is the shape the ruling wanted. Cost: **~5 extra flash judgements** — the entire 4-round
tournament measured 6 flash calls at ~$0.00. **The ranking code needs no change at all**, which is a
far smaller blast radius than rewriting the standing rule.

This is also Co-Scientist's own approach: *"newer and top-ranking hypotheses are prioritized for
participation in tournament matches."*

**REQUIRED TEST, kept exactly as ruled 2026-07-29:** a strong newcomer introduced in a late round
**can still reach the top N**. Without that assertion the rule is unfalsifiable and its failure is
silent — the same shape as ranks 8/9/10 finishing at exactly 1200.0 and taking research slots by index
order. **Under median-seeding this test fails 98.5% of the time, which is precisely why it is kept.**

**An interaction that must be tested together, not separately:** D-R9's fix (raise the rounds) makes
D-R11's problem **worse** — more rounds give incumbents more matches and therefore more wins, and wins
is the primary key. Measured: same newcomer, same rule, at 4 rounds entering round 3 lands **rank 6
(passes)**; at 8 rounds entering round 6, **rank 11**; entering round 7, **rank 16 (fails)**. The
catch-up schedule is what makes raising the rounds safe.

### D-W4-4 — 15.7 ALSO carries the grouping declamp and the aspect-extraction fix

**Operator decision, 2026-07-31.** Two adjacent rulings that had no phase home are **in scope for this
phase**.

**(a) Grouping declamp — Finding 9.** `question_grouping.py:97` reads
`_D6_MAX_GROUPS = min(5, max(1, int(os.environ.get("NESTOR_TRIBUNAL_D6_MAX_GROUPS", "5"))))`. **The
outer `min(5, …)` means the env var can only LOWER the value — setting it to 7 silently still gives
5.** Same trap class as the 240-char cap: a knob that looks like the control and is not.

**Operator ruling (2026-07-30), now homed here:** make **one-group-per-client-question the PRIMARY
path**, keep topic grouping as an option, and **remove the clamp** so the number follows the client.

Two facts that make this cheaper than it sounds. The behaviour **already exists** as
`fallback_groups()`, whose docstring at `question_grouping.py:816` records *"IT IS NOT CLAMPED TO
`_D6_MAX_GROUPS`, AND THAT IS THE ACCEPTED SPEND CONSEQUENCE"* — i.e. exceeding the ceiling was
already shown to the operator and accepted. And for a typical client it is **CHEAPER, not dearer**:
3 questions × 3 providers = **9 calls against a 15 ceiling**. It only overspends past 5 client
questions. The cost is losing topic dedup — shared groundwork gets searched once per question instead
of once per topic.

**(b) Aspect extraction — Finding 6 / exp6.** Make decomposition of a client question into its **N
distinct asks** an **explicit step**, generate against each ask, then have **Python ASSERT** every
aspect has ≥1 sub-question. This matches how the engine already asserts client-question coverage after
grouping — a control, not a request.

Why a prompt tweak is **not** sufficient, measured on the real `claude-sonnet-4-6` generator with the
exact deployed params (3 runs per arm): the deployed prompt produced **16 of 18 compound candidates
(89%)**; adding a coverage rule only reached **12 of 18 (67%)**. **The "use a stronger model" theory
inverts** — on flash the same coverage rule took compound to **0 of 6**, while Sonnet only reached
67%, plausibly because the prompt also says *"never change what is being asked"* and the client's
question genuinely **is** compound, so Sonnet weights parent-fidelity over the format rule.

**The insight that makes this legal:** covering *"fuel retailers in other countries"* is **NOT
broadening** — the client explicitly asked it. `generate_candidates`' SCOPE RULE
(`workshop.py:1059`) is applied so bluntly that it suppresses coverage of the client's **own** asks.
Measured on the real Q1: **3 of 6 candidates named no country at all**, and **orientation named France
and the US as comparators — every candidate dropped both**. So the fix is an **added COVERAGE rule**,
not a relaxed scope lock. The two are compatible, which matters because the scope lock is load-bearing
for D4's coverage guarantees.

*Nuance kept for the executor: never naming the US is arguably correct — orientation cites it only as
a market where supermarkets still win, and the client is Benelux/Germany-focused. **France is the fair
miss.***

### D-R6 / D-R9 — the loop, the tournament, and the arithmetic (spec § 5, unchanged)

**Evolve becomes generative.** Take the top-ranked and produce **new** questions **added to the pool,
not swapping out their parents**: **combine** two winners; **extend** (*"if the answer is yes, what
next?"*); **invert** (*"what would have to be true for this to matter?"*); **specialise** (name the
entity, geography, timeframe). **Delete `workshop_rank.py:1761`** — *"Do NOT merge two questions into
one, and do NOT broaden one."* Keep the scope lock **for the mandate bracket**; discovery questions
are governed by the evidence-anchor rule instead.

**Judges must reason.** Change `_TOURNAMENT_PROMPT` (`workshop_rank.py:995`) output from
`MATCH_INDEX | A` to `MATCH_INDEX | A | <one clause why>`, and pass every judging call the parent
client question **in full** plus the orientation findings for it. Today the judge sees two question
texts, a short decision blurb and a 160-char flaw clause (`_FLAW_MAX_CHARS`,
`workshop_rank.py:171`) — **it is judging blind.** Three effects: better judgements, an audit trail of
*why* 7 beat 9, and material for the meta-review.

**Meta-review.** One call that reads every critique flaw and judge reason for the round and writes
short guidance, fed into the next generation round.

**D-R9 — raise the rounds. CONFIRMED by measurement.** `_TOURNAMENT_ROUNDS = 4`
(`workshop_rank.py:800`) over 17 candidates cannot separate them: each candidate gets only **3.76
matches**. The harness reproduced V-01's exact symptom — **three candidates finishing at Elo exactly
1200.00 with 2 wins each**, straddling the top-10 cut, so one lost its research slot to **index
order**. Raise the rounds so every candidate plays **≥5–6 matches within one loop round** (ratings
persist across rounds, so that qualifier matters). At ~17 candidates that is roughly 6–8 rounds.
**Prefer DERIVING the round count from the candidate count over hardcoding it**, so it cannot silently
under-separate again as the population grows each round.

Measured: **carried Elo + 5 Swiss rounds + the catch-up schedule eliminated the ties entirely — zero
candidates at exactly 1200 in any round.** Rank 10 vs 11 became a decision by **Elo** (1214 vs 1200),
not index order.

**REJECTED, do not reopen:** replacing pairwise Elo with a ranked list plus a run-off. § 5 still
describes that option as *"better at our size"* on its face. **Operator, 2026-07-29: *"we are not
killing tournament".*** Pairwise Elo is retained. Keeping it is also what keeps § 8's
`NESTOR_TRIBUNAL_WORKSHOP_TOURNAMENT=false` A/B control meaningful, and there is only **ONE** measuring
run.

### D-R10 — the loop must DISCOVER, with a CORRECTED admission test

**The ruling stands: the loop must discover, not only sharpen.** Evolve **may invent** an angle in any
round; a cheap grounded lookup admits it or drops it. *"No source, no slot"* is **not weakened** — it
moves from *"only orientation may originate an angle"* to *"only evidence may admit one"*.

**The admission test as originally specified INVERTS its own purpose and MUST change.** Read as *"is
there a published answer to this?"*, it rejected **all 4 invented angles** in the harness run — **zero
survived**. Among the rejected: *"what minimum network density is required for algorithmic pricing to
pay off"* — exactly the strategic question a mid-sized player weighing expansion needs, and exactly
what D-R10 exists to admit. **The rule as written admits angles that are already documented (already
known → low research value) and rejects novel ones (nobody has published it → high research value).
It is a novelty filter pointed backwards.**

**The corrected test: verify the PREMISE is real, not that an answer already exists.** Ask whether the
named entities, markets, mechanisms and metrics **exist**, and whether desk research could plausibly
settle the question — **not** whether someone has already settled it.

> **CRITICAL implementation note, measured, and the harness's own bug is the lesson.** The admission
> evidence **must** come from a real search result — **`groundingChunks`** — and **never** from the
> model's own output line. A looser guard (`if not url`) admitted **2 of 3** angles carrying a literal
> `"-"` as the URL, which is truthy: the model "evidenced" its own angle by tautologically restating
> that its own entities exist. **Read the `groundingChunks`, require an http(s) URL, and treat an
> absent or non-URL source as NOT FOUND.** Without this the grounded lookup is theatre and *"no
> source, no slot"* is enforced by nothing at all.

**Reuse the redirect resolver.** The grounding URL comes back as a
`vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/...` link — **the same redirect class Wave 1's
`D-V01-11` already fixed at ingest.** Reuse that resolver rather than writing a second one.

**Unchanged:** `D-W3-4`'s allocation still bounds what is **dispatched** — ≤5 discovery slots,
per-parent cap 3, never borrows from the mandate. D-R10 widens where candidates may *come from*, not
how many run.

**What measurement says to expect, and it is not what the ruling assumed.** With the corrected
admission test the loop **does** earn its keep — **5 of 10 research slots were loop-generated**. But
**the value comes from the MUTATION moves, not from INVENT**: all five top-10 newcomers were
COMBINE / INVERT / EXTEND, and the single surviving INVENT ranked **16, below the cut** — even though
its content was excellent (*German **Ladenschlussgesetz** exceptions for petrol stations, and which
product categories are legally excluded* — precisely the mechanism underlying the client's whole
premise, and no original candidate touched it). **Genuine discovery arrives via inversion and
combination of existing questions rather than from-scratch invention.** Do not judge the feature by
INVENT's survival rate.

### D-W4-5 — Winner selection: ONE GLOBAL LOOP, floor at the cut, prefer KEEP

**This is `exp11`, the validated configuration. Build exactly this.**

- **ONE global loop and ONE global tournament — NOT per-client-question brackets.**
- **12 candidates generated per client question** — the selection ratio, and the measured lever.
- Winners = a **floor of 5 per client question** + **2 cross-cutting**, applied **at the CUT** rather
  than by splitting the pool into per-question quotas. (Operator ruling 2026-07-30: *5 research
  questions per client question*.)
- **Prefer KEEP over WEAK when filling a slot.**

**Per-question brackets were MEASURED and they fail on every axis.** The operator had ruled for them;
the experiment says no, and the ruling was superseded on the evidence:

| | global (exp7c) | global + floor | **per-question brackets (exp8)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| exits | round 6 | round 6 | **round 10 — THE CAP** |
| cost | $0.18 / 115 calls | $0.18 | **$0.62 / 379 calls (3.4×)** |
| population peak | 23 | 23 | **77 (122 at 5+2)** |
| loop-born winners | 5 of 10 | 4 of 10 | **2 of 9** |
| cross-cutting winners | 2 | 1 | **0** |

**The structural reason underneath all of it: inside a single bracket, evolve cannot COMBINE across
client questions — and cross-question synthesis is where the best output came from.** Brackets also
reintroduce exactly the *"cap every run"* failure the spec feared, and the *"round-9 prompt carrying
60 candidates"* explosion, which **never happens globally**. The population explosion is
**architecture-dependent, not inherent** — the warning is **scoped, not deleted**. If anyone ever
revisits brackets it becomes live again exactly as written.

**`prefer KEEP` is the single highest-leverage rule found all session, and it is one line of selection
logic.** Exit criterion 2 *checks* for `WEAK` winners; **nothing anywhere ever prevented one from
being selected** — a smoke alarm with no fire door. Adding the preference took `WEAK` winners to **0**
and made criterion 2 satisfiable **by construction** rather than by luck.

**Its dependency — the selection ratio — is why the generation count is not cosmetic.** `exp10` and
`exp11` differ in exactly one thing:

| | exp10 — 6 generated | **exp11 — 12 generated** |
|---|---|---|
| the choice at a 5-slot floor | 5-of-6 — **no selection** | **5-of-12 — a real choice** |
| prefer-KEEP | **inert**; no spare KEEP candidates | always has KEEP available |
| winner set | ground clean over 9 rounds | **clean from round 1** |
| exits | round 9 | **round 4** |
| cost | $0.48 | **$0.24** |

**Raising the generation count halved the cost AND more than halved the rounds, at an identical slot
count.** Read as a range these two runs say nothing; read as before/after they identify the lever.

**`exp10` is a SUPERSEDED run with a named defect, not an alternative result. Its round count and cost
must never be quoted as this design's.**

**DO NOT quota discovery per client question.** `discovery_bracket.py` rules against it by name, and
the reason is V-01-grounded: both `brief_conflicts` were about Q1, so a per-question quota would force
the engine to **MANUFACTURE** a coffee discovery question. Discovery stays a **global pool with a
per-parent cap**. **That anti-quota argument does NOT govern the mandate floor** — a mandate floor
manufactures nothing, the per-question candidates already exist. A future reader will cite that
docstring to block the mandate floor; **it does not apply**.

### D-W4-6 — Exit criteria: all three, unchanged, plus two exemptions

**KEEP ALL THREE CRITERIA EXACTLY AS WRITTEN. Measured: the exit rule FIRES.** All three global
configurations exited on the criteria well inside the cap — **exp7c round 6, exp10 round 9, exp11
round 4** — and `WEAK` winners fell **3 → 3 → 0 → 0**. The criteria were observed to **gate each other
in turn** (round 2 saturation passed but quality failed; round 3 the reverse), so the AND is doing
real work rather than blocking forever. **No criterion is removed, weakened or reordered.**

1. **Coverage** — every client question has ≥1 winner rated `KEEP`
2. **Quality** — no `WEAK` question remains in the winner set
3. **Saturation** — the last round's newly-evolved questions produced **no new entrant into the top N**
4. **Hard cap: 10 rounds**, plus **instrumentation** (see D-W4-7)

**Exemption A — a cross-cutting question is compound BY CONSTRUCTION.** It joins two topics *on
purpose*, so the flaw clause *"two questions in one"* must **NOT** count against it in criterion 2.
Without the exemption, criterion 2 structurally penalises exactly the highest-value questions the loop
exists to produce — **it would be built to reject its own best output.** `exp9` marked both its best
questions `WEAK` for precisely this reason.

**Exemption B — resurrected candidates are excluded from criterion 2 (QUALITY).** Two guards keep a
candidate alive when critique tries to kill everything: `_reason_critique_resurrected` (never leave a
client question with zero sub-questions) and `_reason_critique_population` (never empty the
population). **Both are coverage fallbacks, not quality passes.** If a resurrected candidate counts as
a `KEEP`, the loop exits believing it met the quality bar when critique rejected everything.

> **⚠ OFF-BY-ONE, CORRECTED 2026-07-31 — read this carefully.** Until that date, `15.7-OPEN-ITEMS.md`,
> § 5's boxed warning **and** § 8's Wave 4 row all said criterion **1**. That was wrong in a way that
> **inverted its own purpose**: criterion 1 is **coverage** and criterion 2 is **quality**, and
> excluding a resurrected candidate from *coverage* would break the very guarantee resurrection exists
> to provide. All three were corrected in one commit. **The target is criterion 2.**

> **⚠ Half of this guard is already built and half is missing — a Wave 4 implementation requirement.**
> `workshop_rank.py:688` sets `entry["resurrected"] = True` for **Guard 1**. **Guard 2 does not.** At
> `workshop_rank.py:700`ff, when critique kills everything, `_reason_critique_population` rewrites
> every candidate to `KEEP` **unmarked** — so the one case where quality most needs to read as
> *failed* reads as a **perfect pass**. **Mark it there too**, or the exit check still lies in the
> worst case it was written for.

**On hitting the cap with `WEAK` winners still present:** **ship, but record a degradation reason.**
That matches D-12 (*degraded means honest, not broken*). V-01 would have carried *"3 of 10 winners
could not be sharpened past WEAK"* — exactly what an operator wants to see.

**A measured failure mode the implementation must handle:** in the harness, **coverage FAILED in
rounds 4 and 5** before recovering, because the WEAK-after-two-passes barring stripped every `KEEP`
candidate from one client question. The exit AND **correctly refused to exit** — but a real
implementation needs the resurrection/scope guard live on this path.

**If the loop kills too much:** the existing guard injects the client's own question verbatim, ranked
first. Untouched.

### D-W4-7 — Ceilings become INSTRUMENTATION, not enforcement

**Neither the spend ceiling nor a population cap nor a per-round grounded-lookup cap is binding at the
measured scale, so none should be enforced.** Across all three global configurations the population
stayed between **23 and 41** and the largest prompt the loop ever built was **~9k chars**; the
validated configuration costs **$0.24** in total against the spec's original **~$3.00** estimate for
10 rounds.

**Log population and spend per round** — which Wave 5 already collects (`candidates_in`,
`new_candidates`, round cost). **An enforced ceiling nobody has measured a need for is a knob that
will one day truncate a run for no reason; a logged number is what tells you whether a ceiling is ever
warranted.**

**The one guard that does the real work is saturation.** The cap is a ceiling, not a target — **now
CONFIRMED by measurement rather than assumed**. **If runs routinely hit 10, that is evidence the cap
should go HIGHER, not that money is being wasted.**

### D-W4-8 — The truncation caps: raise, do not delete, and there are THREE of them

**`_CANDIDATE_PROMPT_CHARS = 240` (`workshop_rank.py:168`) is the root cause of the entire `WEAK`
epidemic**, and fixing it is one environment variable, not a redesign. Real V-01 candidates are
**245–373 characters**, so **17 of 18 reached the critic cut off mid-word** — 920 characters
discarded, no ellipsis, no question mark surviving — while the critic was asked whether each question
is *"sharp and answerable **AS IT STANDS**"*. It answered honestly about the text it was shown. **It
was never shown the questions.**

| candidate cap | critique result | distinct flaw clauses |
|---|---|---|
| `240` (as shipped) | **KEEP=1 / WEAK=17** | **2** — 16 of them the identical *"two questions in one"* |
| raised | **KEEP=9 / WEAK=9** | many, and specific |

**Two distinct flaw clauses across seventeen rejections is itself the tell.** End-to-end, *"9 of 10
winners `WEAK`"* **reproduces exactly at cap 240 and becomes 2 of 10 with the cap raised** — and the
ranking **reshuffles hard** (#7 rank 1 → 15; #15 rank 13 → 1), so truncation was poisoning the
tournament's judgements too, not just the critique's. `_match_block` truncates **both sides** to the
same 240 chars **and** hands the judge the critique's flaw with *"a flaw counts AGAINST that side"* —
so when the truncated critique gives both sides the same flaw, **that signal cancels to zero**.

> **⚠ THE TRUNCATION IS A REAL SECURITY CONTROL AND MUST NOT SIMPLY BE DELETED.** It bounds
> attacker-influenced text so an injected candidate cannot forge another candidate's output line — the
> same channel Wave 3's `CR-02` closed on `source_url`. **It needs *a* bound; it does not need 240.**
> 240 is simply mis-sized for what the generator produces.

> **⚠ CODE-GROUNDED, AND § 5 NAMES THE WRONG PAIR — verified 2026-07-31 against the live source.**
> The spec warns that the generation prompt *"states the candidate count TWICE"* and that both must be
> changed. **In the repo that is NOT a defect:** `workshop.py:1070` and `:1075` both interpolate the
> **same** `{n}` format variable, sourced once from `_CANDIDATES_PER_QUESTION`
> (`workshop.py:190`, env `NESTOR_TRIBUNAL_WORKSHOP_CANDIDATES_PER_Q`, default **6**). That
> two-authorities defect was an artefact of the **harness's patch**, not of the codebase.
>
> **The real two-authorities defects are different, and there are two of them:**
>
> 1. **`_CANDIDATES_PER_QUESTION_MAX = 10`** (`workshop.py:193`, env `..._PER_Q_MAX`) is the
>    **parse-side hard bound**, applied at `workshop.py:1139` as `out = out[:cap]`. **Raising
>    generation from 6 to 12 without also raising this to ≥12 silently yields 10, not 12** — and the
>    selection ratio is the whole lever of the validated configuration.
> 2. **`_CANDIDATE_MAX_CHARS = 300`** (`workshop.py:196`, env `..._CANDIDATE_CHARS`) truncates each
>    candidate **at parse time**, before it is ever stored. Real candidates run to **373 chars**, so
>    **raising `_CANDIDATE_PROMPT_CHARS` off 240 while leaving this at 300 still hands the critic a

**Also confirmed and NOT caused by the cap:** the **exactly-1200.0 three-way tie that decides research

**Do not "fix" this by enabling thinking.**

### D-W4-9 — The loop gets a MINIMUM-ROUND FLOOR, and it lives inside `exit_verdict`

**Operator decision, 2026-08-04**, closing **gap 1 (the BLOCKER)** and **human_verification item 1**
of `15.7-VERIFICATION.md`. Shipped in commit `8eff424`.

**The loop may not exit before round 4 — `_LOOP_MIN_ROUNDS = 4`** (`workshop_loop.py:72`, env
`NESTOR_TRIBUNAL_WORKSHOP_LOOP_MIN_ROUNDS`), the round at which `exp11` measured convergence.

**WHY A FLOOR IS NEEDED AT ALL, AND IT IS NOT A TUNING PREFERENCE.** Criterion 3 (SATURATION) is
**vacuously true in round 1**: `_stamp_loop_candidates` stamps `born_round = round_no + 1`, so round
1's winner set **structurally cannot contain a loop-born candidate** and `new_entrants` is
necessarily 0. On a KEEP-heavy brief coverage and quality hold too, all three criteria are satisfied,
and the loop breaks after ONE pass.

**What a round-1 exit costs:** no COMBINE ever runs, no cross-question synthesis happens, the
meta-review's guidance is produced and never used, no INVENT candidate ever reaches the evidence
gate, and `select_winners` step 2 finds nothing eligible so the two `_CROSS_CUTTING_SLOTS` are filled
by ordinary single-parent candidates by rank. **That degenerates Wave 4 into the straight line it was
built to replace** — and 15.8's single measuring run is the phase's only evidence.

**THE RECONCILIATION THE VERIFICATION ASKED FOR.** D-W4-5 records `exp11` as **both** "winner set
clean from round 1" **and** "exits round 4". The verification correctly said one of the two accounts
had to be wrong. **Neither is: the floor is what makes them consistent.** A clean round-1 winner set
and an exit at round 4 are the same run once the criteria are ANDed with a floor. The harness had no
floor because it had no such early-exit path to guard.

> **⚠ THE FLOOR IS ENFORCED INSIDE `exit_verdict`, NEVER AT THE `break`.** `workshop_rank`'s
> `if verdict.get("should_exit"): break` reads that one field and nothing else. A round check added
> at the `break` site instead would leave the verdict dict — which the round records, the stage feed
> and the tests all read — reporting `should_exit: true` while the loop kept running. **That is a lie
> in an audited record**, and it is the two-authorities defect class this phase has already paid for
> three times (15.6 `CR-01`, `D-DEF-01`, `D-W4-8`'s `_CANDIDATES_PER_QUESTION_MAX`). **ONE
> AUTHORITY.**

**THE CAP ALWAYS WINS.** `effective_floor = min(floor, cap)`, so at `round_no == cap` the floor is
necessarily satisfied and the driver's `for round_no in range(1, max_rounds + 1)` remains the **sole**
termination guarantee. A floor set absurdly high cannot hang the engine; asserted by
`test_a_cap_below_the_floor_wins_so_the_loop_can_never_be_unterminable`.

**A HOLD IS DISTINGUISHABLE FROM A FAILURE.** The verdict gained `min_rounds` (the **effective**
floor, so the record says what actually applied), `floor_ok`, and `hold_reason`. `hold_reason` is
non-empty **only** when the criteria were met and the floor was not reached; on "criteria not met" it
is empty. **It is NOT a degradation** and stays out of `degradation_reason` — the driver appends that
one to `loop_reasons` as a D-12 degradation, and a loop working exactly as designed must not raise an
alarm (D-12's alarm-fatigue rule).

`test_engine_e2e_stubbed.py` pins `_LOOP_MIN_ROUNDS = 1`. **That is a property of THE FAKE, not of
the engine:** its stub proposes one new question every round, so under the production floor that file
would run to its cap and move its cost, call-count and model-list assertions for a reason that has
nothing to do with the pipeline. `test_workshop_loop.py` owns the floor, at both the unit and the
stage-B seam.

### D-W4-11 — `workshop_notes` are PERSISTED as run events, not only logged
