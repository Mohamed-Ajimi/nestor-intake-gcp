## 2. Task 1 — the loop driver

**Commits:** `d53d98c` (the atomic scope-rule swap), `e4f5389` (the loop).

### 2.1 The scope-lock edit was made ATOMIC, as required

`_EVOLVE_PROMPT` line 2297 — `"- Do NOT merge two questions into one, and do NOT
broaden one."` — was deleted **and its replacement wired in the same commit**
(`d53d98c`). The replacement is a new `{scope_rules}` placeholder filled by
`workshop_rank._scope_rules_block()`, which returns
`workshop_evolve.MANDATE_SCOPE_LOCK` + `workshop_evolve.DISCOVERY_EVIDENCE_ANCHOR`.

Three properties that matter:

- **Both halves are IMPORTED, never retyped.** The sharpening prompt and the
  generative prompt now render the same two constants, so they cannot drift into
  two different scope rules.
- **The import is function-local.** `workshop_evolve` imports `workshop_rank` at
  module level (it aliases `_flatten`, `_normalise_langs`, `_parents_of` and four
  more), so a module-level import the other way does not resolve at all.
- **The failure mode keeps the LOCK.** If the import ever fails, the fallback text
  is the mandate half in plain words — a prompt that silently loses its scope rule
  is precisely the regression the edit had to avoid.

Acceptance greps:

|---|---|---|
| `grep -v '^ *#' workshop_rank.py \| grep -c "Do NOT merge two questions into one"` | 0 | **0** |
| mandate scope-lock replacement present | ≥ 1 | **1** (`MANDATE_SCOPE_LOCK`) |
| `DISCOVERY_EVIDENCE_ANCHOR` present | ≥ 1 | **1** |

### 2.2 THE DECISION: `evolve_winners` vs `evolve_generative`

**Stated in the code at the top of the loop, and here.**

| step | when | job |
|---|---|---|
| `workshop_evolve.evolve_generative` | ONCE PER ROUND, inside the loop | **GENERATION** — grows the population via the five moves. Never touches the winners it was given. |
| `evolve_winners` (this module) | ONCE, after the loop, over the final chosen winners | **SHARPENING** — final wording, and D7 `langs`. |

**`langs` is why the second one survives at all.** It is written only by
`evolve_winners` via `_normalise_langs`, and plan 15.2-13 builds its angle-query
language sentence only when `langs` is non-empty. A loop that routed around it
would ship D7-less winners while every other assertion in this phase read green.

### 2.3 `born_round` is the round the candidate FIRST COMPETES IN

This is the subtlest thing in Task 1 and it is a latent always-passes-criterion
bug if got wrong.

`exit_verdict` tests criterion 3 (SATURATION) as `born_round == round_no` over the
winners **selected in that round**. Selection happens *before* evolve inside a
round, so a candidate written by round N's evolve can never appear in round N's
winner set. Stamping it `N` would make `new_entrants` permanently zero and
criterion 3 permanently TRUE — a criterion that always passes is not a criterion,
and the loop would exit the first time coverage and quality happened to align.

`_stamp_loop_candidates` therefore stamps `round_no + 1`, and says why.

### 2.4 Observed exit-round range

Driven through the REAL `run_workshop_stage_b` with scripted stubs, five seeds,
identical configuration:

```
observed exit rounds: [3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 4]      (six seeds)
winners: 17 for 3 client questions  (5 + 5 + 5 + 2 — the validated shape)
```

Asserted as a **RANGE** (`1 <= r < 10`, plus `r >= 2`), never as a constant. The
`r >= 2` half is load-bearing: without it a run that exited in round 1 would
satisfy the range assertion while proving nothing about the loop.

**A real finding while building this.** The first scripted brief exited at round 1
for every seed, and that turned out to be *correct behaviour*, not a bug: with a
KEEP-heavy population, prefer-KEEP satisfies coverage and quality immediately and
saturation passes trivially in round 1 (nothing carries a `born_round` yet). To
exercise the loop at all, one client question's candidates must be WEAK for the
first rounds so its floor slots have no KEEP to prefer. That is now written into
the test's docstring, because a future reader tuning the fixture back to
all-KEEP would silently reduce the loop test to a one-round test.

## 3. Task 2 — the register, the admission gate, the instrumentation

**Commit:** `bf278b9`.

### 3.1 THE KILL SPLIT RULE, and its failure direction

**The rule as implemented** (`workshop_rank._kill_is_a_restatement`):

> A killed candidate is treated as a **RESTATEMENT** — and therefore never barred
> — when it is demonstrably part of a near-duplicate family: it absorbed members
> (`merged_from` non-empty), or another live candidate carries the same non-empty
> `cluster_key`. Every other KILL is a **DEFECT** and bars with
> `BAR_KILL_DEFECT`. **If there is no clustering signal at all, nothing is
> barred.**

**Why not read the flaw text.** The critique prompt defines KILL as four things at
once — *"unanswerable in principle, pure opinion, A RESTATEMENT OF ANOTHER
CANDIDATE, or nothing about the client's decision turns on it"* — and the obvious
implementation is to match the word "restatement" in the flaw clause. That is the
exact trap `workshop_loop._exempt_cross_cutting` already documents in capitals: the
flaw clause is **model prose in the run's own language**, so a text matcher would
silently NEVER FIRE on a Dutch or French run, and a guard that never fires is worse
than no guard because it reads as a solved problem. A restatement of another
candidate is precisely what near-duplicate clustering already detects, so the
signal used is structural and language-independent.

**THE FAILURE DIRECTION IS TOWARDS NOT BARRING**, as the plan required:

- no clustering signal → **nothing barred** (the safe default, not an oversight);
- clusterer misses a restatement → one restatement barred, costing one duplicate
  the clusterer would have collapsed anyway;
- the failure being avoided is the **over-eager** bar, which suppresses discovery
  *invisibly* — nothing errors, the round simply produces less — and that is the
  failure the Wave 4 harness actually measured.

### 3.2 A signal that did not exist and had to be added

`critique_candidates` **discards killed candidates entirely** — they are removed
from `survivors` and their named `flaw` goes with them. A caller could see *that*
something was killed (by diffing the input) but never *why*, so the KILL split had
no input at all.

It now takes an optional `killed_out` out-list — the same additive out-parameter
idiom the function already uses for `stats`, so no existing caller changed. Two
properties are load-bearing:

- each entry carries `{index, text, flaw, parents, cluster_key, merged_from}` —
  the flaw *and* the clustering shape, which is what the split needs;
- **a RESURRECTED candidate is reconciled OUT of the list.** Both critique guards
  put a killed candidate back into `survivors`, and barring a candidate the
  pipeline chose to keep would delete exactly the coverage resurrection exists to
  provide (T-15.7-09-02). Done once, inside `critique_candidates`, rather than at
  each call site.

### 3.3 The three bars, and the one that is structural

| cause | trigger | where |
|---|---|---|
| `BAR_KILL_DEFECT` | a KILL that is not a near-duplicate family member | loop step 1a |
| `BAR_WEAK_TWICE` | `note_weak_pass(...) >= 2` — **two** evolve passes, never one | loop step 1b |
| `BAR_LOOKUP_FAILED` | every angle `admit_invented_angles` dropped | loop step 5a |
| *a tournament loss* | **NEVER** | structurally impossible |

**Losing never bars, and the enforcement is structural rather than a rule anyone
has to remember**: `workshop_register.bar` accepts exactly three causes and "came
last" is not one of them, so the loop could not bar a loser even by accident.

### 3.4 The `round_no=0` hazard, avoided deliberately

`cluster_candidates` is called with the **real loop round number**, never the `0`
default plan 07 gave it. Stamping every drop round 0 would destroy the one
distinction the drop log exists to make — *"round 2 re-proposed round 1's rejects"*
(the loop SPINNING) versus an over-eager dedup killing novel angles — and that
failure is silent with every test green. Written into the code comment at the call
site.

### 3.5 A CONTRADICTION IN THE PLAN, reported rather than silently resolved

**Task 2 asks for two things that cannot both hold literally.**

- `<behavior>`: "an admitted one **joins the discovery pool carrying its quote and
  URL**";
- `<action>`: "then into the **EXISTING `allocate_discovery` call**. Do not change
  `discovery_bracket` and do not change D-W3-4's allocation."

`allocate_discovery` **does not read an incoming `text` at all.** It composes one
via `discovery_bracket.discovery_question_text`, whose fixed frame reads *"The
brief assumes: X. A source read during orientation says instead: Y (url)."*
Pushing an admitted invention through it would:

1. **discard the question the INVENT move actually wrote** — the one thing the
   whole D-R10 admission gate was paid for; and
2. **assert it came from orientation**, which is false. It came from the loop, and
   its source is its own admitting lookup. That sentence would reach the client in
   the report.

**Resolution taken:** the allocation is **continued, not re-run**.
`allocate_discovery` runs first over the real orientation conflicts and returns its
per-parent counts; `_fill_remaining_discovery_slots` then fills admitted angles into
the slots orientation left unused, under the **same ceilings read from
`discovery_bracket`** (`_DISCOVERY_MAX_SLOTS`, `_DISCOVERY_PER_PARENT_CAP`) rather
than retyped. `discovery_bracket` is not modified, there is still exactly one set of
numbers, and D-W3-4's bound is the bound that binds. Both the adapter and the
filler carry the reasoning in their docstrings.

**This is a judgement call on an under-specified point and should be reviewed.**

### 3.6 Mutant — both columns, and it did NOT behave as the plan predicted

| mutant | baseline | mutant |
|---|---|---|
| **C — bar on a TOURNAMENT LOSS** (every below-cut candidate barred with an accepted cause) | `BARRED=12 WINNERS=17 VERBATIM=0 PROMOTED_WITH_RANK=17` | `BARRED=45 WINNERS=17 VERBATIM=0 PROMOTED_WITH_RANK=17` |

The mutant is **live and detectable** — bars rise from 12 to 45 — but **the winner
set is unchanged**, and that matters.

The plan's acceptance criterion says *"a mutant that bars on a tournament loss
makes the promotion test in Task 3 fail"*. **On this architecture it does not, and
Task 3 should not be written expecting it to.** A bar affects two things: the
`barred_block` rendered into later prompts, and the semantic drop of **newly
proposed** candidates in `cluster_candidates`. It does **not** remove an
already-live candidate from `ranked`, so `enforce_scope_guard`'s promotion ladder
still finds and promotes a below-the-cut candidate in the same run.

The real damage from barring losers is therefore **in later rounds** — the loop
stops exploring anything resembling a barred question — and the detectable signal
is the register size and the drop log, not the promotion. Task 3 can still assert
the guarantee (losers remain promotable), but it should get its non-vacuity from a
mutant that **removes the candidate from `all_ranked`**, not from one that bars it.

### Harness note, for whoever runs Task 3

The lift harness lives in the session scratchpad, not in the repo. Two properties
were necessary and are worth re-creating rather than rediscovering:

- the stub finder must be **appended** to `sys.meta_path`, never inserted at the
  front — inserting it shadowed `typing`'s own dependencies and produced a
  metaclass conflict *inside `typing`*;
- the engine submodules that drag in `anthropic` / the audit client
  (`reliability` excepted — it is stdlib-only and worth lifting for real) must be
  **pre-registered as stubs in `sys.modules`**, because they exist on disk and
  would otherwise load for real.

**not** `anthropic_messages`. A stub missing that method does not fail loudly — every
verdict silently falls back to KEEP/default, which is what made the first
scripted brief exit in round 1 and looked like a loop bug.
