<objective>
Fix the compound-question epidemic at generation (D-W4-4b) and build the second enforcement layer of
the rejected register (D-W4-1).

Measured on the REAL `claude-sonnet-4-6` generator with the exact deployed parameters, 3 runs per arm:
the deployed prompt produced **16 of 18 compound candidates (89%)**; adding a coverage rule to the
prompt only reached **12 of 18 (67%)**. A prompt tweak is proven insufficient. And the "use a stronger
model" theory INVERTS — on flash the same coverage rule took compound to **0 of 6** while Sonnet only
reached 67%, plausibly because the prompt also says "never change what is being asked" and the
client's question genuinely IS compound, so Sonnet weights parent-fidelity over the format rule.

So the fix is a Python ASSERTION over an explicit decomposition, exactly as the engine already asserts
client-question coverage after grouping. A control, not a request.

The insight that makes it legal: covering "fuel retailers in other countries" is NOT broadening — the
client explicitly asked it. The SCOPE RULE at `workshop.py:1059` is applied so bluntly that it
suppresses coverage of the client's OWN asks. Measured on the real Q1: **3 of 6 candidates named no
country at all**, and **orientation named France and the US as comparators, and every candidate
dropped both**. The fix is an ADDED COVERAGE RULE, not a relaxed scope lock — and the two are
compatible, which matters because the scope lock is load-bearing for D4's coverage guarantees.

Purpose: this is where the loop stops being fed compound questions in the first place, and where the
barred list stops being a polite request.
Output: `workshop.py` with an aspect step, a coverage assertion, a barred-aware clusterer, and a drop
log that records what clustered onto what.
</objective>
