# Phase 15.7 Plan 02: Raise the Five Workshop Constants — Summary

Raised the five constants that between them produced the entire `WEAK` epidemic — generation
count 6→12, parse-side bound 10→24, stored candidate width 300→600, global cap 60→120,
prompt-side candidate width 240→600 and evolved-winner width 400→600 — with every comment
re-derived from the measured before/after rather than having its number swapped, and one test
that asserts the whole ladder and its ordering relations at once.

## What Was Built

**Task 1 — the four generation-side constants (`workshop.py`).** Defaults only; every env var
name is byte-identical to what it was. The comment block was rewritten so each number carries
its reason: 12 is the *selection ratio* (a 5-of-12 choice instead of a 5-of-6 non-choice, which
is what makes prefer-KEEP-over-WEAK non-inert); 24 is the parse-side hard bound that must stay
strictly above the generation count or asking for twelve silently yields ten; 600 is the width
`research_division._SUBQ_CHARS` already sends the same text to three paid providers at; 120 is
`12 × 10` client questions, where the old 60 was exactly `12 × 5` and would have started eating
the selection ratio at the sixth client question.

**Task 2 — the two prompt-side widths (`workshop_rank.py`).** `_CANDIDATE_PROMPT_CHARS` 240→600
and `_WINNER_MAX_CHARS` 400→600. The comment keeps and expands the SECURITY CONTROL statement —
the bound is *raised, never deleted* — and now records what 240 cost with the measured numbers,
the second-order `_match_block` effect where an identical truncation-induced flaw on both sides
cancels to zero, and the correction that thinking-disabled is **not** the cause and must not be
"fixed" by enabling thinking. `_FLAW_MAX_CHARS` stays at 160 with its reason written down.

**The ladder test.** `test_the_candidate_width_and_count_ladder_moves_as_one` in
`test_workshop_tournament.py` asserts all five values *and* the four relations that must hold,
plus that every width is still a finite positive bound below 5,000. Its negative arm,
`test_the_raised_bound_is_still_a_bound`, drives the real `_flatten` and proves a 5,000-character
candidate is still cut and that newlines and pipes still collapse before truncation.

## Deviations from Plan

### Auto-fixed Issues

**1. [Rule 1 — Bug in the plan's own instruction] `test_workshop_critique.py:656` was NOT rewritten
to read `workshop_rank._CANDIDATE_PROMPT_CHARS`**

- **Found during:** Task 1
- **Issue:** The plan lists `:656` as one of "the two sites hardcoding 240" and instructs both to
  read `workshop_rank._CANDIDATE_PROMPT_CHARS`. That site is
  `test_clustering_reuses_the_15_1_clusterer`, and its `c['text'][:240]` mirrors
  **`grouping._cluster_block`** (`grouping.py:263`, an inline literal with no named constant),
  not the workshop candidate ladder. This plan does not move grouping's width. Wiring the
  assertion to `_CANDIDATE_PROMPT_CHARS` would have coupled it to the wrong authority: with the
  constant at 600 and grouping still slicing at 240, the test would have kept passing only
  because the fixtures are ~40 characters — i.e. silently vacuous, and wrong the moment either
  side moved.
- **Fix:** Dropped the slice entirely and added an explicit guard,
  `assert all(len(c["text"]) < 120 …)`, so the no-slice shortcut cannot silently rot if someone
  lengthens the fixtures. The docstring now says which width belongs to whom. The acceptance
  criterion "no literal `240` outside a comment in `test_workshop_critique.py`" is satisfied.
- **Files modified:** `tribunal/nestor_pulse_sdk/tests/test_workshop_critique.py`
- **Commit:** `ce1d07d`

**2. [Rule 2 — Missing critical coverage] Added `test_asking_for_n_candidates_actually_yields_n`**

- **Found during:** Task 1
- **Issue:** Nothing in the suite asserted the round trip the whole selection ratio depends on —
  that N asked-for lines actually survive the parse-side bound. The two constants were only ever
  compared to themselves.
- **Fix:** New test in `test_workshop_critique.py` that drives the real
  `_parse_candidate_lines` with `_CANDIDATES_PER_QUESTION` lines and asserts both that all N
  survive and that the parse-side bound stays strictly above N.
- **Commit:** `ce1d07d`
