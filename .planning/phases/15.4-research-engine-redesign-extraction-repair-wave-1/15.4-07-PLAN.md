`cost_prices.json` — keys are `provider/model`; values are USD PER 1M TOKENS as
`{prompt, completion, cache_read, cache_creation_5m}`. Loaded by `audit/cost_table.py::_load_prices`
(`COST_PRICES_PATH` env override, `_DEFAULT_PRICES_PATH` at line 58). There is no `gpt-5` entry of
any kind today: the openai block holds `gpt-4o`, `gpt-4o-mini`, `o4-mini`, `o4-mini-deep-research`,
`o3`, `gpt-4-turbo`.

THE TRAP, AND IT IS THE WHOLE POINT OF THIS PLAN. Read `compute()`'s `_rate()` helper
(cost_table.py:230):

    val = entry.get(field)
    if val is None:  -> log a warning and return Decimal("0")

So an entry added WITH NULL RATES does not produce "unknown". It produces a confident, wrong
**$0.00**, and `cost_pending` clears on a fabricated number. Whereas an ABSENT key takes the
`key not in prices` branch, returns None, and the caller writes NULL `cost_usd` + `cost_pending` —
which is the honest state.

Therefore there are exactly two legal outcomes: PUBLISHED RATES, or NO ENTRY. "Add the key with
nulls" is not a compromise between them; it is worse than either.

The file's own `_tool_fees_source` note sets this standard already: "null here means 'no
plan-independent published rate exists', NOT '$0.00'".
</interfaces>
