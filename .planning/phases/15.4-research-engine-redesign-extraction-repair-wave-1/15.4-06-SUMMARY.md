## The three answers

### Q1 — the funnel reconciles exactly, residual 0

`426 distilled − 27 refuted − 3 conflict losers = 396 persisted.` `293` is not on that line at all: it
is the gate-*kept* count, a different cut of the same 426. Gate-dropped claims are **not** removed from
the funnel — they stay in `claims`, are adjudicated, and are persisted. That misreading is what made
the question look unanswerable.

Each integer measured independently:

| Number | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 426 | 11 materiality gate calls in the bucket; answered index ranges sum to 10×40 + 26 |
| 293 | 8 stability gate calls; 7×40 + 13 |
| 27 | 255 `emit_group_verdict` blobs → 302 claims, 1 verdict each (`support 182 · insufficient 86 · refute 27 · superseded 7`); replaying `adjudicate()` drops exactly 27 |
| 3 | conflict-detector blob `92457a42` — 5 conflicts, 3 with a non-null `loser`, 2 contested |
| 396 | 426 − 27 − 3 |

`rejected_claims` therefore holds **30 entries** (`pipeline.py:3166-3175`). The row itself was not
read; its recorded 9,189 chars over 30 entries is 306 chars/entry, the right order for the JSON shape
— corroboration, not proof.

Five candidate mechanisms named in the plan were tested and **ruled out** (`_dedupe_claims`,
`unusable_claims`, `_NOT_FOUND_TOTAL_MAX`, `_MAX_FACTS`, gate drops) — the first four sit upstream of
the 426; the fifth does not remove claims from the funnel.

**One residual, stated:** 302 claims carry a verdict against a recorded `checked: 293`. The 9-claim
gap is consistent with `checked_incidentally` (`pipeline.py:996-1002`) but was **not verified** — gate
requests are truncated to 2,000 chars in the audit blob, so a claim cannot be mapped to its gate
decision from the bucket.

**Scope: nothing to fix, nothing entered the phase.**

### Q2 — `gate_errors: 153` is a response-format rejection, and no operator was warned

All 153 are in gate 1 (materiality); gate 2 produced **zero**. Not one is an API failure — all 11 gate
calls returned `finish_reason: STOP` with a full-length answer. The model answered in a one-column
shorthand where the contract (`gates.py:166`) requires two columns, and `_parse_gate_lines` rejects a
partially valid row whole (`gates.py:245`, `gates.py:254-256` — both deliberate and both correct).

Every rejected line, by shape: `110 × 'N | KEEP'`, `32 × 'N | NOT_LOAD_BEARING'`,
`6 × 'N | NOT_FALSIFIABLE'`, `5 ×` reason-written-into-the-decision-slot. Total 153. **Same class of
provider format drift as the `<TAB>` defect, in a different stage.**

**Increment site:** `gates.py:492`. Flag set at `gates.py:239/258` (silent), `gates.py:437-440`
(silent), `gates.py:466-467` (silent), `gates.py:337-342` (the only logged origin — did not fire).

**Cost:** the default is KEEP+VERIFY, so the direction is safe. But **43 of the 153 were the model
explicitly saying DROP with a reason**, and those 43 were sent to paid skeptic sessions against the
gate's own judgement. 43 of 293 ≈ 15% of a verification stage that carried $51.89 of the run's $53.48
→ roughly **$6–8** — an estimate from the proportion, not a per-call measurement.

**Did anything tell a human? Almost — and that is the finding.** The number *was* displayed:
`pipeline.py:2517-2528` renders `"… · 153 gate errors (sent for checking)"` into the gate feed row with
`status: "done"`, and `ResearchRunProgress.tsx:325` prints it verbatim. But:

- `gates.py:514` is `log.info` — **inert in production** (D-V01-6, WARNING and above only);
- `gates.py:337` WARNING never fired (no batch failed);
- `pipeline.py:2543` ERROR never fired (threshold is `gate_errors >= distilled`; 153 < 426);
- and the one sentence written to say this out loud — `report.py:277`, *"153 claim(s) were sent for
  checking on a defaulted gate answer."* — **was never rendered**, because `_degradation` returns early
  at `report.py:256-257` on a run whose `should_have_been_checked` is 0.

A third of the gate stage failed its contract, no log line at WARNING or above existed, and the feed
said `done`.

**Fix proposed in writing** (accept the shorthand; warn on a short-parsed batch; un-gate the
sentence). **Scope: OUT OF SCOPE, deferred.** Wave 1 is the extraction funnel; changing gate parsing
changes which claims get paid verification, which is the exact variable Wave 1 exists to hold still so
the next live run measures the `<TAB>` fix and nothing else.

### Q3 — disposition **(b)**: a report-writer change, out of scope

**`certainty` never reaches the report writer in any form.** Traced through every input of
`synthesize_report` (`steps.py:863-874`): the anchor ledger carries `{anchor, prefix, claim_id, text,
facet}` (`anchors.py:222-230`), built from `list_run_claims`, which selects `{claim_id, text, facet,
position}` (`numbering.py:334-341`). A null-certainty claim is **by construction** indistinguishable
from a `certain` one.

Further: `certainty` has a writer (`extractor.py:318`) and **no reader** outside dedupe and
normalisation. It is a write-only column, and the design intent recorded at `facts.py:98-100` — that a
weak certainty *"sends the claim to the skeptics"* — is **not implemented**. So a null certainty costs
nothing relative to a stated one, because a stated one also does nothing.

**Report quote (required by the acceptance criteria).** A distiller-path claim (`certainty = NULL` by
construction, `steps.py:1941`, T-15.2-61) and the sentence it became:

> claim: *"Middelgrote brandstofretailers in de Benelux hebben tussen 2019 en 2024 dynamische
> prijsstelling (dynamic pricing) geïmplementeerd."*
> report: *"Middelgrote brandstofretailers hebben tussen 2019 en 2024 dynamic pricing geïmplementeerd
> `[[c:a1c41b2e]]`."*

Flat assertion with a citation anchor — identical treatment to any `certain` claim.

*Provenance caveat, stated in the document:* this is the report **writer's response** from the audit
bucket, the direct antecedent of the `output` row with `format='markdown'`. The `output` row itself was
not read, because no database credentials were used.

**Options framed for the operator, with a recommendation and no decision taken:** (A) accept as-is;
(B) mark certainty in the report — *do not build*, 44% of claims would be marked NULL and the marker
would mean "no provider volunteered a word", not "weaker evidence"; (C) mark the **skeptic verdict**
instead — our own check rather than a provider self-report. **Recommended: A now, C later.**

## Adjacent finding — recorded, deliberately not acted on

The same trace answers a bigger question than the one asked. Of the 302 verdicts measured,
**86 came back `insufficient`**. `adjudicate()` drops only on majority-refute-with-an-independent-source,
and `scrub_research` removes passages only for *discredited* claims — so all 86 survive, and since the
ledger carries no verdict either, they read to the client exactly like corroborated claims. **Roughly
28% of what the Tribunal checked came back "we could not establish this" and the report says so
nowhere.** Recorded for the report-composition wave; **explicitly not pulled into Wave 1.**

expires with the next run. It is not the reason recommended above.
