<interfaces>
READ THIS BEFORE TOUCHING ANYTHING — CONTEXT corrects the spec here.

`emit_safe(run_id, *, stage, kind, build)` (runs/run_events.py:402) takes a ZERO-ARGUMENT CALLABLE
precisely so a malformed provider dict raises INSIDE its `try` instead of at the call site in the
paid dispatch loop. Its docstring spells this out as D-06 and warns, in words:

    "ONE try/except wraps BOTH the build() call and the emit(...) that follows it. DO NOT 'tidy'
     this by assigning build() to a local above the try -- that hoists the evaluation back out of
     the protected region and reintroduces the entire defect while looking correct."

It caught these. The run was unaffected. The EVENTS were still lost, because the lambdas raise.
So: fix the lambdas. NEVER loosen `emit_safe`, NEVER hoist `build()` above its try, and do not add a
second try/except at a call site — one helper is checkable with one grep, thirty wrappers are not.

Site 1 — `research_division.py` ~1188, inside `_record_result`:
    build=lambda: (f"Angle {i + 1:02d} done — {len(result['facts'])} facts · {provider}", {...})
`result['facts']` is a SUBSCRIPT and its comment says so deliberately (T-15.3-23): a `.get`
defaulting to 0 would print "0 facts" for an angle whose fact count is simply UNKNOWN — a feed row
asserting something the run never established. THAT REASONING STANDS. The fix is not `.get(…, 0)`.

The same file at ~1376 shows the accepted tolerant form for `agent_run`, with a comment naming the
asymmetry: `.get` here and NOT a subscript, unlike the `agent_done` line below.

Site 2 — `own_researcher.py` ~776:
    build=lambda: (f"Own query done — {len(facts)} facts from {len(citations)} pages · "
                   f"{_raw_fact_count(fact_block) - len(facts)} skipped", {...})
`_raw_fact_count` (own_researcher.py:349) RAISES BY DESIGN — its docstring: "a skipped count that
cannot be computed must not be printed as zero." That reasoning also stands. `len(facts)` and
`len(citations)` are locals that are always countable; only the skipped term can fail.
</interfaces>
