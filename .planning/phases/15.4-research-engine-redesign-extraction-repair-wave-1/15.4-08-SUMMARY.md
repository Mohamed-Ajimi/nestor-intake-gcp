# Phase 15.4 Plan 08: The WARNING that would have caught V-01 — Summary

**One-liner:** `_distill_unit` now says at WARNING — the lowest level production
serves — that the model returned N lines and the parser kept none of them, naming the
provider and the first offending line; and the ZERO-claims warning next to it stopped
crying wolf about facets that were never in the call.

## What this plan is for

Plan 15.4-03 fixed the parser: the 278 discarded coffee claims now parse, proven in CI by
`test_the_two_coffee_calls_sum_to_the_278_the_client_never_saw`. **This plan is why nobody
noticed for a month.** The only trace of 278 dropped claims was a `log.debug`, and per
D-V01-6 stdlib logging in this pipeline is served by Python's `lastResort` handler, which
starts at WARNING — DEBUG and INFO do not exist in production. A future format the parser
does not anticipate must be LOUD. That is the deliverable; the parser fix is the previous
plan's.

## Task 2 — D-R1(d): scope the ZERO-claims warning (commit `ffd676a`)

`steps.py` lines **1714–1756**. `in_scope` is built from `_angle` on the call's own
`provider_reports`; the loop warns only for focus areas in that set. On V-01 the old loop
walked the whole mission brief and reported zero for `convenience` — a facet in a different
report, one that parsed its fact list successfully and never entered the distiller — printed
right beside the real coffee zero.

The **degradation** is written out because it is the part the spec snippet omits: when no
report carries a usable `_angle`, `in_scope` is empty and the scoped loop would warn about
nothing at all, trading a false alarm for total silence. It falls back to the full brief and
logs once, at WARNING, that the warnings beneath it are unscoped:

```
claim_distiller: facet scoping UNAVAILABLE for this call — no provider report carried an
_angle, so the 2 ZERO-claims warning(s) below are checked against the whole mission brief
and may name focus areas that were never in this call (D-R1(d))
```

Seven tests in `TestZeroClaimsWarningIsScoped`, asserting the **SET** of facets warned about,
extracted from `record.args[0]` rather than substring-matched. A test that counted warnings
would have passed against the V-01 defect — two warnings, one about the wrong facet, is
still two warnings.

`facet_counts` and the closing INFO summary are unchanged, asserted directly
(`test_facet_counts_and_the_closing_summary_are_unchanged`).

## Deviations from Plan

### One deliberate narrowing, argued rather than assumed

**The scoping-unavailable notice fires only when there is a ZERO warning to qualify.**

The plan's behaviour bullet reads "*falls back to the full focus-area list (today's
behaviour) AND logs once that scoping was unavailable*", which read literally means an
unconditional notice on every `_angle`-less call. Two reasons it is conditional instead:

1. **It is more faithful to "today's behaviour", not less.** When `in_scope` is empty and no
   facet is at zero, today's code emits nothing. The conditional emits nothing. An
   unconditional notice would be a NEW log line on a path that previously said nothing —
   and it would fire on every `full_extraction=False` safety-net call whose reports carry no
   angle, including all of `test_distiller_coverage.py`.
2. **The notice's only content is a qualification of the warnings under it.** With no
   warnings under it, it qualifies nothing. Announcing a scoping failure with nothing beneath
   it is a brand-new false alarm inside the fix for false alarms — the exact behaviour
   D-R1(d) exists to remove.

The plan's stated acceptance criterion ("with every `_angle` blank, the number of ZERO
warnings equals today's behaviour AND one additional scoping-unavailable WARNING is
present") is met unchanged — that scenario has facets at zero. The narrowing is pinned by its
own named test, `test_the_degradation_notice_is_silent_when_there_is_nothing_to_qualify`,
whose docstring records the reasoning so a later reader meets it as a decision rather than
an omission.

### Two small refinements inside the plan's own action text

- The plan's snippet logs `(text.splitlines() or [""])[0][:200]` — the first line. The
  behaviour spec above it says "the first **non-empty** line". The behaviour spec won: a
  response beginning with a blank line would otherwise log `''` and tell the reader nothing.
  The line count and the logged line are read from the same `non_empty` list, so they can
  never disagree.
- A whitespace-only response is truthy, so it warns with a count of `0`. That is honest —
  something came back and nothing survived — and the `0` distinguishes it at a glance from
  the 141-line case.
