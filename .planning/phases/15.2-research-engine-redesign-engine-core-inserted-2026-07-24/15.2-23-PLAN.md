<objective>
**D-I — personal data was dispatched to third-party research providers.** And **D-M — Gemini ignored
the D8 fact-list instruction on 8 of 8 reports.**

**D-I, exactly what happened.** Angle #6 of run `d6bb3aae` was a paid Google deep-research
assignment, dispatched from the parent *"Primary client contact: MEEMZ (mohamed.ajimi@azentic.be) —
role still to be filled in"*, asking:

> *What is the exact job title and organisational role of Mohamed Ajimi (MEEMZ) within or towards
> LUKOIL — internal strategy lead, external consultant, or other?*

A named individual's email address left the platform to an external processor, as a research task,
serving no client decision. **Treat as a data-protection incident, not a quality bug.**

Plan 15.2-21 removes the *cause*: with only the client's validated questions in the workshop
population, that parent — a stakeholder line from context-pack §9 — never becomes a question and the
angle is never generated. This plan adds the **defence in depth** the findings ask for: even if input
selection regresses, even if a client writes an email address into a research question, **no query
leaving this engine carries a personal identifier**. That belongs at the dispatch choke point, where
every provider call funnels through one line, not at the input, where there are many.

The engine already has the right idiom twice over: `reliability.redact` strips credentials from
anything that reaches a log or the operator feed, and
`audited_llm_client.strip_unresolved_cite_markers` returns `(text, count)` so a silent removal
becomes a reported one. `scrub_pii` is the third sibling in that family.

**D-M, exactly what happened.** Five times over at 09:09:43:

    facts: gemini returned no FACTS_START/FACTS_END block — its report will be run through
    the full-extraction distiller instead

then `collect_provider_facts: gemini returned no usable fact list for 5 of 8 research report(s)`.

**The good news is load-bearing and must not be lost: 15.2-14's D-14 fallback worked live, first
time.** Every report without a usable block was routed through `claim_distiller` in full-extraction
mode, exactly as designed. Nothing in this plan may weaken that path — it is the reason the run still
produced claims.

**The honest limit of this plan.** Whether Gemini *complies* with an instruction is a live-LLM
question, and no live run may be spent to verify it (the Anthropic org is on a temporary burner key
and cost discipline forbids it). So this plan does NOT claim to fix Gemini's compliance. It ships the
three things that ARE provable offline and that make the next live run measurable rather than
another guess:

1. the instruction is stated where a long-context research agent will still see it — a short
   REQUIRED-OUTPUT lead-in for Gemini, with the full block still at the tail, because a
   deep-research agent composes its report from a summarised prompt and a 2.1 KB block at the very
   end is the easiest thing to lose;
2. the placeholder-URL rejection (`facts: rejecting non-http(s) SOURCE_URL 'N/A'` was observed live)
   becomes a NAMED list with a test, instead of an accident of the `http(s)` check;
3. the honour rate is already measured — `collect_provider_facts` logs it per provider and in
   aggregate — so the SUMMARY records where to read it after the next run rather than adding a
   second counter inside `synthesis/steps.py`, which is the D-15 file and is not worth entering for
   a nicer log line.

Purpose: close a data-protection incident at the egress point, and stop paying twice for every
Gemini report.
Output: `pii.scrub_pii`, the dispatch-point scrub on both hops, provider-aware fact-list placement, a
named placeholder-URL reject list, and two test modules.
</objective>
