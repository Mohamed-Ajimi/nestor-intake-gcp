<the_headline_trap>
**F5 — `_MAX_ANGLES = 12` and a trimmer that eats corroboration first.**

`research_division.py:68` caps angles at 12. `research_division.py:187-201` handles the overflow by
building `primaries` (everything that is *not* a high-stakes redundancy copy) and keeping
`primaries[:_MAX_ANGLES]`, backfilling with redundancy copies only if room remains. Under today's
engine that is correct: the redundancy copy is optional extra coverage.

Under D6 it is **exactly backwards**. The duplicate copies of a top-ranked sub-question across four
streams ARE the corroboration signal — the thing `grouping.group_claims` clusters and
`_group_corroboration` (`pipeline.py:154`) counts to decide checking priority. A trimmer that drops
them first deletes the merge's evidence base at source and leaves the run looking healthy.

Both halves must change:
- the cap must be large enough that a normal run never trims at all (arithmetic in Task 1), and
- the trim ladder must sacrifice **surplus depth** before **corroboration**, and record every trim.
</the_headline_trap>
