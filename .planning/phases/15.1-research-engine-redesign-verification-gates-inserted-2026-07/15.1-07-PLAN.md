<objective>
Wire the gates into the verification stage and take stakes out of the selector's seat (G-02).
Today `_group_passes(stakes)` returns 0 for low-stakes groups, silently waving them through
unchecked — a second hidden filter behind the one nobody can see. After this plan the gates
are the single answer to "which claims get checked", stakes survives only as the depth lever,
and every claim that should have been checked but was not is counted (G-08 bucket 3).

Purpose: one honest funnel. This is also where G-04's ordering lands — cluster, then gate per
claim, then let a cluster survive if ANY member survives, then prioritise by corroboration.

Output: the gate stage inside `pipeline.py`, a gate-driven selector, corroboration-ascending
queue order, an explicit depth tier for low-stakes survivors, and bucket-3 counters at all
three loss sites.

**Two decisions this plan must make deliberately, not by accident:**
1. `_GROUP_DEPTH` has NO `"low"` key, so a low-stakes group that now survives the gates would
   silently get MED depth via `.get(stakes, _GROUP_DEPTH["med"])` — a real cost increase against
   the "~6× cheaper" bar (RESEARCH Pitfall 9). Task 2 adds an explicit shallow tier.
2. `triage_claims` is called from exactly one place — the `NESTOR_TRIBUNAL_GROUP_VERIFY=false`
   fallback branch. RESEARCH recommends REWIRE, not delete: that branch is also the vehicle for
   `_one_skeptic`, which the coverage-gate re-entry uses in BOTH modes.
</objective>
