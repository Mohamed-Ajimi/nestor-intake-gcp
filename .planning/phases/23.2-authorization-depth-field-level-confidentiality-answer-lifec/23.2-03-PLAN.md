## THE ORDERING, EXACTLY (D-23.2-09)

For BOTH `deactivate_space` and `reactivate_space`:

1. Resolve the space (404) and read memberships — unchanged.
2. Run the existing guards — unchanged. On deactivate: the self-lockout 409 and the

3. Compute `targets` — unchanged. Deactivate's `targets` must KEEP including already
   `space_deactivated` rows (that inclusion is the retry mechanism; `:598-601` says so).
4. **Call the IdP** for every target with a `provider_user_id`. `_apply_to_idp` already never
   returns early and already returns the failed uids.
5. **Then** flip the DB, for the members whose IdP call did NOT fail. A target with a NULL
   `provider_user_id` has nothing to disable and is flipped (unchanged behaviour — say so in a
   comment, because "we skipped the IdP" and "the IdP failed" must not be conflated).
6. Flip the space row. Keep flipping it even on partial failure: `organizations.status` is

   `members_restored` must count the rows ACTUALLY flipped, not `len(targets)`, or the audit
   trail inherits the over-claim you are removing).
8. On failure: `repo.session.commit()` then the 502, unchanged in shape and message. The commit
   before the raise is load-bearing — the existing comment explains why and it stays true.

**Why this is safe in the crash window**: a crash between step 4 and step 8 leaves members
disabled at the IdP with `active` rows. They are denied. That is fail-CLOSED, and it is the

fix this — widening it to `!= _STATUS_ACTIVE` would sweep up the individually `deactivated`
members and undo T-23.1-08. The ordering is the whole fix.

## D-23.2-10 — THE TWO SPACE-ACTIVE GUARDS

* `invite_user` (`admin_routes.py:148-200`) checks the space EXISTS but never that it is
  ACTIVE, so today you can invite an active member into a deactivated space. Add the check
  immediately after the existing `get_space` 404, BEFORE the duplicate-membership 409 and
  BEFORE any IdP call. **409**, not 404 — the caller is a superadmin who legitimately sees the
  space, and hiding it would be a lie about a resource they can list.
* `reactivate_user` (`admin_routes.py:271-301`, the INDIVIDUAL verb) restores a member with no
  reference to their space's status, so it can hand access back inside a deactivated space. Read
  the membership's `organization_id`, load the space, and 409 if it is not active. Place it
  after the membership 404 and BEFORE `admin_users.reactivate_user` — no IdP call, no flip, no
  audit row on the refused path.
* `deactivate_user` (the individual DEACTIVATE, `:218`) needs NO guard. Deactivating a member
  of a deactivated space is a safe, idempotent narrowing. Do not add one.
