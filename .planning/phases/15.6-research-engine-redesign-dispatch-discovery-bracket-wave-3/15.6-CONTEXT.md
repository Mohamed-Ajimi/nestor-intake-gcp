<domain>
## Phase Boundary

**What this phase delivers.** Research stops being dealt out by *position* and starts being dispatched
by *topic*, and the engine gains the right to ask a question the client did not.

Today `_divide_from_winners` sends the **top-3 winners to all four streams** and **deals the remainder
round-robin, one angle each** (`research_division.py:867-874`). That is why V-01's coffee question did
not land on gemini because gemini is good at Benelux retail — it landed there by **position in the
deal**. And the one place preference *is* applied is inverted: `_STAKES_PROVIDER` routes **high** stakes
to gemini, the least format-reliable provider on V-01, and reserves claude, the most reliable, for
**low** stakes.

**What replaces it.** An LLM groups the winners into **at most 5 groups** by shared research
groundwork, and **each group goes to all three providers**. Grouping is **semantic, not structural**: a
group may span two client questions where they overlap, and one client question may split into two
groups if it is really two topics. That is where the saving comes from — shared groundwork is searched
once instead of five times.

**Plus a discovery bracket.** Questions the *evidence* raised, each carrying the quote and source that
provoked it. **No source, no slot.**

**Why this is not "more corroboration" — do not justify it that way.** V-01 measured **2.9% of URLs
cited by ≥2 providers** and **37 of 396 claims** with any cross-provider partner at a very loose
Jaccard 0.2. Four providers on one question largely read four different corpora. The real payoff is
**failure independence and complementary reach**: coffee got three sub-questions at one provider each,
so when two hit the `<TAB>` parser bug the client's entire coffee question survived on 8 claims from a
single provider. Under grouping, one provider failing leaves two standing.

**No routing logic at all.** Uniform allocation is correct *because there is no trustworthy data to
route on* — V-01's yield numbers are contaminated by the `<TAB>` bug. Wave 5 (15.8) collects the data
that could justify routing later. Until then uniformity is the honest choice, and it deletes the
inverted stakes→provider map outright.

**What Wave 2 (15.5) unlocked, and what this phase owes back to it.** 15.5 stamps `sub_question` and
`corroboration_key` on the claim row, and today `corroboration_key` is NULL for ~12 of 15 winners
because only the top-3 are dispatched as corroboration copies. **Under grouping the group IS the key** —
every group goes to every provider, so every claim gets one and the column finally populates. Wave 2's
`facet`-from-sub-question resolution (`claim_attribution.resolved_facet` / `parent_index`, currently
with **no production caller**) is what makes a group spanning two client questions legal. **This phase
is its first production caller.**

**NOT DEPLOYED.** Operator ruling 2026-07-29 — *"I don't want to measure anything unless we finish all
changes."* Waves 1, 2 and 3 land in git only. **ONE** deploy and **ONE** measuring run happen at the end
of phase 15.8. Do not add a deploy step to any plan. Do not propose one.

### D-W3-1 — Group count: the LLM proposes, Python clamps, **hard maximum 5**

**Operator decision, 2026-07-29** (given as a direct instruction: *"could be less than 5 but max 5"*).

§ 4 asked for "a **default dial**, not a hard cap … let it rise for complex briefs". **The operator
overrode the rising half.** Five is a **ceiling**, not a target:

- The grouping LLM returns however many groups the material actually has.
- Python clamps to **≤ 5**. **Fewer is allowed and expected on a simple brief.** There is no floor
  padding — do not invent groups to reach 5.
- **The spec's headline 5 × 3 = 15 calls is therefore a CEILING, not a fixed cost.** A 3-group brief
  costs 9. Against V-01's 19, the saving is at least 4 calls and may be more.
- **The discovery group counts INSIDE the 5** (see D-W3-4), so the mandate gets ≤ 4 groups whenever
  discovery fires, and all 5 when it does not.

**The consequence § 4 warned about is accepted.** With a hard ceiling, a brief with 7 genuinely
distinct topics gets one grab-bag group. That was the operator's call; record it, and make run one able
to show it (see the group-size dial below).

### D-W3-2 — Grouping failure: **one group per client question**, degraded, and the old machinery is **deleted**

**Operator decision, 2026-07-29.**

Grouping is a new paid LLM call on the critical path, and an LLM that groups is an LLM that can return
nothing usable. On failure or unusable output:

- Fall back to **one group per client question**, each dispatched to all three providers.
- Record a **D-12 degradation reason** so the delivered report says so. Follow the existing D-12
  convention in `workshop_rank.py` — note its distinction between a *note* and a *degradation*: a
  partial repair is a NOTE because the output is still complete; a FULL fallback is what degrades.
  This is a full fallback of the grouping step, so it **degrades**.
- **DELETE the top-k + round-robin machinery.** `_D6_TOP_K` and the remainder deal in
  `_divide_from_winners` are subsumed and must not survive as a second, unreachable dispatch strategy.
  The deterministic fallback above replaces them.
- **`NESTOR_TRIBUNAL_D6_TOP_K` becomes a dead env knob.** Remove it rather than leave it readable and
  inert — an env var that no longer does anything is a trap for whoever tunes the 15.8 run.

**Two things to keep straight.** `divide()` has TWO paths and only ONE is being replaced
(`research_division.py:884-931`): `winners` truthy → the D6 branch `_divide_from_winners` (**this is
what changes**); `winners` falsy → the original focus-area path, which is the *workshop*-fallback and is
**byte-behaviour identical, covered by its own seven tests, and out of scope here.** Do not touch it.

**Accepted spend consequence, stated explicitly because it breaks the ceiling.** The fallback is
capped by *client-question count*, not by 5. Six client questions means 18 calls — above the 15-call
ceiling D-W3-1 sets for the happy path. This was shown to the operator and accepted: on the degraded
path, covering every client question beats holding the spend line. Note it collides with T-15.2-61
(*angle count is the only real spend control this engine has left*, since the budget governor is inert
under `NESTOR_TRIBUNAL_UNCAPPED=1`) — so the fallback must **log the call count loudly** when it
exceeds the happy-path ceiling.

### D-W3-4 — Discovery bracket: 1 group, ≤ 5 slots, **per-parent cap 3**

**Operator decision, 2026-07-29.**

- **Mandate bracket** — the client's questions and their sub-questions. Coverage guaranteed exactly as
  today. **Nothing here can be displaced by a discovered question, ever.**
- **Discovery bracket** — **one group, at most 5 questions**, each carrying the quote and source that
  provoked it. **No source, no slot.** It can never borrow from the mandate. **Unused slots roll back
  to the mandate** (more depth), never into more discovery.
- **Allocation is a global pool with a per-parent cap of 3.** NOT a per-question quota: on V-01 *both*
  `brief_conflicts` were about Q1, so a per-question quota would have forced the system to
  **manufacture** a coffee discovery question to fill it — exactly the free invention that was ruled
  out. **A quota forces invention.** The per-parent cap exists because discovery volume partly tracks
  research volume (Q1 had 8 reports, coffee 3), so a pure global pool quietly rewards the
  already-well-funded question.
- **The accepted risk of 3 over 2:** three of five slots may come from one parent, i.e. discovery can be
  60% dominated by the best-funded question. The operator chose the looser cap deliberately, because
  that is also where the evidence genuinely concentrates. **Log the per-parent distribution** so run one
  can show whether it bit.

**Cross-cutting discoveries need a home.** A finding like *"two chains bought 300 Benelux sites in
2025"* bears on pricing *and* coffee *and* convenience. Every candidate must carry a `parent` — D4's
assertion joins on it, and `research_division` derives stakes and allocation from `rank` alongside it.
Give cross-cutting questions an explicit parent value **`__discovery__`** that the coverage assertion
knows to **IGNORE**. Otherwise the guard either rejects them or, worse, silently counts one as covering
a client question it does not answer.

**Timing — this is what makes discovery affordable.** Discovery can only spend slots *this run* if it
fires **BEFORE** research, i.e. off the **orientation pass**, which already produces `brief_conflicts`
(`workshop.py:613`, `:646`, `:687`; consumed at `pipeline.py:1742`). Richer discoveries surface from the
research itself (442,904 chars on V-01) but by then the money is spent — those become **"questions for
the next run"**, reported and not researched.

**Governance — discovery cannot be gated on a click.** `D5 / D-01` states the workshop is **FULLY
AUTOMATIC**: *"Nothing in this module pauses for an operator."* So discovered questions instead carry
their provenance into the report, landing in the section that already exists —
`"### Where the brief did not match what the research found"` (`synthesis/steps.py:532`, populated at
`:709-717`). The client sees plainly which questions they asked and which the evidence raised.
**Provenance is also required for the Art. 12 audit trail — deadline 2026-08-02.**

### D-W3-5 — **Mandate strict, discovery rides along.** One ruling, both brackets

**Operator decision, 2026-07-29**, taken after the plans were first written. **This overrides an
explicit sentence of spec § 4** (*"A group may span two client questions where they overlap"*) and
resolves an ambiguity § 4 never settled — its diagram feeds `winners (mandate + discovery)` into one
grouping step, while its worked comparison lists `Discovery … ~1 group`. **The rule below is
authoritative over both.**

**Why the ruling was needed.** The primary fact-list path has **no per-fact facet column** —
`facts.py:298` is `STATEMENT<TAB>SOURCE_URL<TAB>QUALITY<TAB>CERTAINTY<TAB>EVIDENCE` — so `facet` is
stamped in Python from the angle (`steps.py:2400`) and passed at **three** `fact_source="fact_list"`
call sites — verified at `:2435`, `:2498` and `:2570`, the last being the D-R2 corrective re-ask, the
branch phase 15.5 only found via a blind mutation. (The `distiller_fallback` site at `:2625` hardcodes
`facet="general"` and is a different path.) **Line numbers drift — find these by symbol, not by
number.** **Everything sharing a group shares one attribution.** The seeded FACET at `steps.py:1627` is
the `distiller_fallback` path only and does **not** correct this — do not write that it does.

**The rule:**

1. **A mandate group contains members from exactly ONE client question.** Never two — *unless* the
   ≤5 ceiling makes single-parent arithmetically impossible (i.e. **more than 5 client questions**).
   Only then may a mandate group span two, and every such group is flagged and warned.
2. **A discovery question parented to a client question label MAY join that label's mandate group.**
   That is where its shared groundwork already is, and it costs nothing extra.
3. **A cross-cutting discovery question (`__discovery__`) gets its own group.** If there are none, no
   discovery group exists and **discovery consumes no slot at all** — which is why this is the
   cheapest option: V-01's 3 questions land at **9–12 calls**, not 15.
4. D-W3-4's allocation is unchanged and independent of which group carries a question: **≤5 discovery
   slots, per-parent cap 3, no source no slot, never borrows from the mandate.**

**The consequence, accepted deliberately:** a discovery claim files under its **host** question's
facet. That is tolerable precisely because `__discovery__` is **not** a client question — so no client
question can read `0` in `claims_per_facet` because of it — and discovery provenance reaches the
client through its own report section regardless. **Client-question counts stay exact, which is the
number the 15.8 run is judged on.**

**THE REFINEMENT THIS FORCES — do not miss it.** Discovery members now sit **inside** mandate groups,
so the coverage assertion must count **MANDATE MEMBERS**, not mandate *groups*. A group whose only Q2
content is a discovery question parented `"Q2"` must **NOT** satisfy Q2's coverage — otherwise the
client's own question can go unresearched while a question the evidence raised stands in for it. This
is strictly stronger than "ignore `__discovery__`", and stronger than the group-level rule that was
correct before this ruling.

**And the warning must not cry wolf.** A group holding one client question plus a discovery question
is the **intended** shape, not a defect — the mixed-group `log.warning` fires **only** when a group
holds **two different client questions**, never for mandate + discovery. See
[[gate-integrity-traps]]: the ZERO-claims warning that cried wolf about facets the call never saw is
half of why nobody noticed V-01's 278 lost claims.

### The four safety requirements from § 4 — all still binding

1. **MOVE THE COVERAGE ASSERTION.** `enforce_scope_guard` (`workshop_rank.py:2024`) today asserts the
   winners' `parents` UNION covers every client question. It must **now also assert every client
   question is represented in at least one GROUP**, in Python, **after** the LLM groups. *An LLM
   deciding grouping is an LLM that can drop a question.* Keep the existing repair ladder exactly:
   promote the label's best-ranked below-the-cut candidate, else inject the client question verbatim,
   and place repairs **at the TOP, ranked first**. That placement is load-bearing and documented at
   `:2045-2053` — stakes and stream allocation derive from `rank`, so appending at the bottom gives the
   client's own validated question the weakest treatment. The function is **idempotent and never
   raises**; keep both properties.
2. **CAP QUESTIONS PER GROUP.** The known risk is a provider writing six thin paragraphs instead of one
   deep report. V-01 gives mild reassurance — gemini's r8 covered Shell, Circle K *and* LUKOIL in depth
   in one report — but **that is one data point.**
3. **A rule for >5 natural groups** — settled by D-W3-1: there isn't one, 5 is a hard ceiling.
4. **Attribution** — delivered by Wave 2 (15.5). Non-negotiable, and now consumed here.

### Claude's Discretion

- **The group-size cap number** (§ 4 requirement 2 — the spec asks for a cap and gives no value). Make
  it an env-backed dial in the style of the existing `_D6_*` constants, defaulting to **4**. Arithmetic
  that must hold: `_D6_MAX_WINNERS` is **15** and D-W3-1 caps groups at **5**, so a cap below 3 is
  infeasible and 4 leaves slack.
  **State the precedence explicitly in code:** when the LLM under-groups and a group exceeds the size
  cap, deterministically re-split by rank *only while the ≤5 ceiling permits*; when it does not,
  **the ceiling wins — accept the oversized group and log loudly.** D-W3-1 is an operator decision and
  the size cap is mine; mine yields.
- Plan count and wave structure. This phase is substantially bigger than 15.5 — do not compress it into
  too few plans, and respect that `research_division.py` and `workshop_rank.py` are each touched by
  more than one concern.
- The grouping prompt's wording and its tool/schema shape, following the existing
  `EMIT_ORIENTATION_TOOL` pattern in `tools.py:254` and `force_emit_orientation()` at `:324`.
- Where the grouping call lives (a new module beside `workshop.py`, or inside it).

One single-quoted `bash -c '…'` block, capped at **10,000 chars** (~5,150 now). **No apostrophes

  exact sets, on any file two plans in this phase both touch — and `research_division.py` will be
  touched by more than one.
- **`gcloud builds submit … | tail` reports exit 0 on a FAILED build.** Read the build text or
  `gcloud builds list`, never the shell status.
